The manifesto

A life we
can actually
live.

Our anger has a reason. We have been told to stay quiet, stay grateful, stay in our place. Fuck that.

We want bodies we decide about. Work that leaves room for living. Relationships we can leave without punishment. A world where nobody needs permission to exist.

Bring your art, your anger, your doubts, your willingness to help. We can disagree, learn, and change. Our freedom grows when we protect one another’s freedom.

Let the fury become work we can share: make something, organize, care for one another. There is room here to begin again.

An open project by Gio Bertuccelli.

What we stand for

Freedom
belongs
to everyone.

You don’t need the right vocabulary, a perfect past, or a party membership. Start with respect for another person’s right to live and choose.

Your life is yours.

Your body, gender, sexuality, and conscience belong to you. Everyone else deserves the same freedom, including people you disagree with. We oppose systems that assign anyone fewer rights because of who they are or where they come from.

Love is not a debt.

Abusive relatives are not owed your love, loyalty, forgiveness, or continued access. Culture and tradition do not excuse cruelty. You decide what relationships you keep, what you leave, and what you carry forward.

Nobody inherits the right to rule.

We reject patriarchy and religious authority over other people’s lives. Every belief can be questioned. Equal rights cannot depend on obedience to a family, a religion, a nation, or a leader.

Equal freedom, everywhere.

We oppose Zionism as a political ideology, occupation, and ethnic supremacy. Our standard is equal rights, freedom, and safety for Palestinians and Israelis. We defend people’s dignity across ethnicity and religion.

Our lives outrank their profit.

We oppose corporate oligarchy and an economy that treats people and the living world as disposable. Wealth must not buy political power. We stand for workers’ freedom to organize, shared prosperity, and democratic control over the decisions that shape our lives.

A launchpad, open to everyone

Give the dawn
something to do.

Art, shared knowledge, mutual aid, and nonviolent organizing. Start with what you can make, what you can learn, or who you can stand beside.

Make something.

A song. A film. A poster. An honest account of your own life. Give an idea a form someone else can feel.

Bring an idea

Learn together.

Read the people who resisted before us. Question the stories we inherited. Share what helps people understand and act.

Show up.

Support a worker organizing. Help a neighbor. Stand beside someone being excluded. Turn solidarity into something they can count on.
